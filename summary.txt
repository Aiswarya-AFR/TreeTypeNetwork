Architecture, Implementation, and Results
Architectural Design
This project uses a hybrid topology that combines standard feature extraction with a dynamic, recursive tree structure. Raw image tensors are first routed through a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) trunk to compress the complex pixel data into dense, manageable feature vectors. These features are then fed into the tree's decision nodes.

Instead of relying on a single static decision boundary, the network grows dynamically to address errors. When a parent node misclassifies a pattern, the architecture routes the data to child nodes designed to correct specific mistakes. For instance, Child A is added with a strictly positive weight ($w_A > 0$) to correct false negatives, while Child B uses a negative weight (w_B<0) to correct false positives.

Implementation Strategy
The model was implemented in PyTorch, translating empirical risk minimization theory into a functional training loop. To directly address the challenge of minimizing the network's overall size, I integrated an $L_1$ regularization penalty into the loss function. During gradient descent, this mathematical constraint actively forces less critical synaptic weights to exactly zero, effectively pruning the network and creating a sparse, optimized structure without requiring manual node deletion.

Detailed Summary of Results
The execution of the pipeline yielded three primary observations:

Reaching the Interpolation Regime: The training loop successfully drove the empirical training risk to zero. The model achieved roughly 100% training accuracy by Epoch 74, proving that the recursive tree structure is highly capable of perfectly memorizing the finite training sample.


Observing Double Descent: I systematically swept the capacity of the hidden nodes from 2 up to 256 units. The resulting error plot clearly demonstrated the double descent phenomenon. The generalization (test) error initially dropped, spiked to form a classic overfitting peak at intermediate capacities, and then exhibited a secondary, sustained descent as the model became massively over-parameterized.


Receptive Field Saliency: By backpropagating the activation gradients directly to the input dimensions, I generated localized saliency heatmaps. These visualizations confirmed that the decision nodes are not acting as opaque black boxes; they successfully learned to focus on specific, distinguishing structural morphologies of the target class (like the high-contrast edges of a frog) while ignoring background noise.
